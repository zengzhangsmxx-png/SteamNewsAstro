## Getting Started in Dead by Daylight

Dead by Daylight presents new players with an asymmetrical horror experience where four survivors attempt to escape while one killer hunts them down. The core objective for survivors involves repairing five generators to power exit gates, while killers must prevent this by sacrificing survivors to The Entity. Understanding these fundamental mechanics forms the foundation for improvement in both roles.

As a new player, you'll face a steep learning curve with 30+ killers and dozens of maps to master. The game's current player base averages 50,000-60,000 concurrent users according to Steam statistics, ensuring quick matchmaking times. However, success requires patience and practice, as the game's skill-based matchmaking system places beginners against players of similar experience levels.

Your first 10-15 matches will determine your initial matchmaking rating, making early performance crucial for balanced gameplay experiences. Focus on learning core mechanics rather than winning every match during this calibration period.

## Essential Survivor Gameplay Fundamentals

### Generator Repair and Objective Management

Generator repair serves as the primary survivor objective, requiring 90 charges to complete each generator. Skill checks appear randomly during repairs, demanding precise timing to avoid alerting the killer. Failed skill checks create loud noise notifications and regression penalties of 10% progress.

Multiple survivors can work on the same generator, reducing completion time through efficiency bonuses. Two survivors complete generators in approximately 44 seconds, while solo repairs take 90 seconds. However, grouping creates vulnerability to killer abilities that target multiple survivors simultaneously.

Prioritize generators strategically by creating a "three-generator" situation where remaining generators are spread across the map. Avoid repairing generators in close proximity, as this allows killers to patrol efficiently and prevent completion.

### Stealth and Movement Mechanics

Movement creates different audio and visual cues that killers can detect. Walking produces minimal noise and no visual indicators, while running generates scratch marks visible to killers for 10 seconds. Crouching eliminates most detection methods but significantly reduces movement speed.

Windows and pallets provide escape routes during chases. Vaulting windows takes 1.5 seconds and creates noise notifications. Pallets can be dropped to stun killers for 2 seconds, but each pallet can only be used once per match.

| Action | Detection Risk | Speed | Best Use Case |
|--------|----------------|--------|---------------|
| Walking | Low | 100% | Map traversal, stealth |
| Running | High | 150% | Chase situations |
| Crouching | Minimal | 46% | Hiding, avoiding detection |

### Healing and Health States

Survivors have two health states: healthy and injured. Injured survivors move at the same speed but create audible groaning sounds and leave blood trails. Healing from injured to healthy takes 16 seconds with another survivor's help or 32 seconds using self-healing perks.

Medical kits provide faster healing speeds and charges for multiple uses. Brown medical kits offer 24 charges, while purple kits provide 32 charges with additional bonuses. Understanding healing efficiency helps optimize survival time during matches.

## Killer Basics and Hunting Strategies

### Understanding Killer Powers and Movement

Killers move faster than survivors at 115% speed for most killers, with some exceptions like Huntress at 110%. This speed advantage means survivors cannot outrun killers in straight lines, requiring strategic use of obstacles and terrain.

Each killer possesses a unique power that changes gameplay approaches. The Trapper places bear traps to control areas, while The Nurse can teleport through walls. Learning individual killer powers helps predict their behavior and plan appropriate counter-strategies.

Attack mechanics include basic attacks with 2.7-second cooldowns and special attacks unique to each killer. Basic attacks put survivors into the dying state after two hits, while some special attacks can instantly down healthy survivors.

### Map Control and Patrol Patterns

Effective killer gameplay requires efficient patrol routes that cover generator locations while minimizing travel time. Most maps contain 7 generators with 5 requiring completion, creating strategic decisions about which areas to defend.

Hook placement becomes crucial for map control, as carrying survivors takes time and limits killer mobility. Basement hooks provide stronger security but require longer travel distances on many maps. Understanding each map's layout improves both generator defense and hook placement decisions.

## Advanced Techniques for Both Roles

### Looping Fundamentals for Survivors

Looping involves running around objects in predictable patterns to waste the killer's time. Basic loops include jungle gyms, T-walls, and shack structures found on most maps. Successful looping requires understanding window vault timings and pallet drop decisions.

The killer's "red stain" indicates their facing direction, allowing survivors to predict movement and plan escape routes. Advanced players manipulate this information by using moonwalking and other techniques to confuse killer predictions.

### Perk Selection Strategy

Dead by Daylight features over 100 perks for each role, creating numerous build possibilities. Beginner-friendly survivor perks include Bond for teammate coordination, Spine Chill for killer detection, and Iron Will to reduce injury sounds.

Killer perks focus on information gathering, generator regression, and chase assistance. Barbecue & Chili reveals survivor locations after hooking, while Hex: Ruin automatically regresses generators. Understanding perk synergies creates more effective builds than random selections.

## Common Beginner Mistakes and Solutions

New survivors often make critical errors that lead to quick elimination. Running constantly creates scratch marks that experienced killers follow easily. Instead, alternate between walking and running based on the killer's proximity and line of sight.

Attempting risky rescues without proper timing or preparation frequently results in multiple survivor casualties. Wait for the killer to leave the hook area and approach carefully to avoid detection. Coordinating with teammates through perks or communication prevents multiple survivors from attempting the same rescue.

Killer players commonly focus too heavily on individual chases, allowing other survivors to complete generators freely. Learning when to abandon chases and apply map pressure improves overall performance significantly. A 30-second chase that results in one generator completion trades poorly compared to finding easier targets.

## Practice and Improvement Pathways

Dead by Daylight's complexity requires dedicated practice to master both roles effectively. Playing both survivor and killer provides valuable perspective on game mechanics and opponent strategies. Understanding how detection works as a killer improves stealth gameplay as a survivor.

Custom games with friends allow practice without matchmaking pressure. Many community content creators offer educational gameplay footage and strategy guides for specific killers and maps. Behavior Interactive regularly updates game balance through patches, making continued learning essential for long-term success.

The game's progression system rewards consistent play through bloodpoint earnings and character level advancement. Each character reaches level 50 with unique perks unlocking at levels 30, 35, and 40. Planning character progression helps optimize perk availability for preferred playstyles.

Success in Dead by Daylight comes from understanding core mechanics, practicing positioning and timing, and adapting strategies based on opponent behavior. Focus on steady improvement rather than perfect performance, as even experienced players face challenging matches regularly.
